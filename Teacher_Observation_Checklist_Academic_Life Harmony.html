Detailed Teacher Guide

Academic-Life Harmony Behavioral Criteria

1. Workload Pacing & Time Management

Indicator 1.1: Breaks long-term projects into manageable daily/weekly goals.

What to Look For: The student creates an outline, checklist, or schedule with intermediate milestones (e.g., draft completed by Tuesday, peer review by Thursday) instead of trying to finish everything in one sitting.

Red Flag: The student leaves 100% of the project untouched until 24–48 hours before the final due date.

Indicator 1.2: Submits assignments on time during standard school/day hours.

What to Look For: Digital submissions arrive during normal waking hours (e.g., between 8:00 AM and 8:00 PM). Work submitted in class is complete upon arrival.

Red Flag: Online submissions consistently timestamped at 2:00 AM, 3:00 AM, or minutes before a midnight deadline.

Indicator 1.3: Uses a planner, journal, or digital tool to track task deadlines.

What to Look For: The student actively references a notebook, planner app, or calendar during class when new assignments or project milestones are announced.

Red Flag: Relying entirely on memory, resulting in frequent questions like "Wait, was this due today?" or missed deadlines.

2. Boundary-Setting & Capacity Management

Indicator 2.1: Expresses personal workload capacity clearly and politely to peers.

What to Look For: During group work, the student speaks up about what they can realistically accomplish within the given timeframe without overpromising.

Red Flag: Aggressively agreeing to do everyone else’s work out of fear of conflict, or conversely, refusing to contribute at all.

Indicator 2.2: Establishes healthy limits on group communication.

What to Look For: The student sets clear expectations with group members (e.g., "I do not check group messages after 8:00 PM because of family dinner, but I will review everything at 7:00 AM").

Red Flag: Continuous messaging in class or after hours; high anxiety about missing instant messages from teammates.

Indicator 2.3: Focuses on assigned roles without overcommitting or taking over others' work.

What to Look For: The student completes their designated portion with care and allows peers to complete theirs, stepping in to offer assistance only when asked.

Red Flag: Micro-managing teammates, re-doing a peer's work completely, or taking over the entire group project out of perfectionism.

3. Mindful Disconnection & Focus Transitions

Indicator 3.1: Puts away unnecessary digital devices during focused class activities.

What to Look For: Phones are placed inside bags or designated areas during lectures, group discussions, and independent work time without needing constant reminders.

Red Flag: Secretly checking social media, texting, or switching tabs on laptops during instruction.

Indicator 3.2: Takes restorative pauses during breaks.

What to Look For: During recess or between classes, the student stands up, stretches, drinks water, talks with friends, or steps outside for fresh air.

Red Flag: Staring at a smartphone screen continuously through every single break, leading to digital eye strain and mental fatigue.

Indicator 3.3: Transitions smoothly from academic focus during class to relaxation during recess/breaks.

What to Look For: The student actively engages in social interactions or rest during breaks without obsessing over grades or unfinished work.

Red Flag: Expressing visible physical tension, panic, or relentless pacing during leisure times over an upcoming task.

4. Proactive Stress Management & Help-Seeking

Indicator 4.1: Seeks help or clarification early when encountering academic roadblocks.

What to Look For: The student asks questions in class, visits the teacher during consultation hours, or consults peers as soon as a concept becomes confusing.

Red Flag: Remaining silent when confused, giving up completely, or waiting until after failing a quiz to mention they didn't understand the material weeks ago.

Indicator 4.2: Uses healthy coping mechanisms during high-pressure tasks.

What to Look For: The student uses controlled breathing, positive self-talk, or organized step-by-step checklists when feeling overwhelmed during exams or presentations.

Red Flag: Showing extreme panic, freezing up, shutting down emotionally, or expressing severe negative self-talk ("I'm going to fail no matter what").

Indicator 4.3: Maintains composure and constructive focus when project plans or deadlines change.

What to Look For: The student adapts calmly to unforeseen schedule changes, group restructuring, or revised instructions.

Red Flag: Experiencing intense frustration, outbursts, or complete disengagement whenever an unexpected change occurs.

5. Self-Care & Energy Preservation

Indicator 5.1: Arrives at school alert, well-rested, and ready to participate.

What to Look For: The student maintains consistent energy levels, active participation, and posture during morning and afternoon classes.

Red Flag: Frequently sleeping on desks, complaining of chronic headaches/fatigue, or relying heavily on energy drinks to get through the school day.

Indicator 5.2: Maintains daily well-being routines.

What to Look For: The student regularly carries a water bottle, eats lunch during breaks, and participates in physical movement during physical education or movement breaks.

Red Flag: Skipping meals to work on homework during lunch periods or neglecting basic physical health for academic work.
