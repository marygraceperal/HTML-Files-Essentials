1. Wages vs. Salaries

"Good morning/afternoon, class! Before we dive into the math, let's make a clear distinction between how people get paid[cite: 12]. Imagine two different workers: a service crew member at a fast-food chain and a school principal[cite: 12]. The service crew member earns an hourly wage[cite: 12]. If they work 5 hours, they get paid for 5 hours[cite: 12]. If they work 8 hours, they get paid for 8[cite: 12]. Every single minute counts! On the other hand, a salaried worker receives a fixed annual salary[cite: 12]. Whether their workweek is slightly lighter or heavier, their base paycheck stays steady[cite: 12]. Today, our focus is on wage earners because tracking their exact time is critical to computing their fair pay[cite: 12]."

1. Wages vs. Salaries
  • Hourly Wage: Payment calculated based directly on the actual number of hours worked[cite: 12].
    • Common Jobs: Construction workers, service crew, freelancers, factory staff[cite: 12].
    • Key Requirement: Accurate tracking of work hours is essential for precise compensation[cite: 12].
  • Annual Salary: A fixed total compensation paid over the course of a year, regardless of the specific number of hours worked[cite: 12].
2. Regular Pay Foundations & The Labor Code

"Now, let's look at the baseline established by the Philippine Labor Code[cite: 12]. The law sets standard full-time work at 8 hours per day, which typically totals 40 hours across a 5-day workweek[cite: 12]. Calculating basic regular pay is straightforward: simply multiply the worker's hourly rate by their regular hours worked[cite: 12]. For instance, if a worker earns ₱70 per hour and completes a standard 8-hour shift, they earn ₱560 for that day[cite: 12]. Over 5 days, that accumulates to ₱2,800[cite: 12]. This standard 8-hour rate forms the base layer of our gross income calculation[cite: 12]."

2. Regular Pay Foundations & The Labor Code

Under the Labor Code of the Philippines, standard work duration is 8 hours a day or 40 hours a week[cite: 12].

$$\text{Regular Pay} = \text{Hourly Rate} \times \text{Regular Hours}$$[cite: 12]
Example:
  • Hourly Rate: ₱70.00/hr[cite: 12]
  • Standard Daily Work: 8 hours[cite: 12]
  • $\text{Daily Regular Pay} = \text{₱70.00} \times 8 = \text{₱560.00}$[cite: 12]
  • $\text{Weekly Regular Pay (5 Days)} = \text{₱560.00} \times 5 = \text{₱2,800.00}$[cite: 12]
3. Overtime (OT) Pay Principles

"What happens when an employee stays past their standard 8 hours? That’s where Overtime (OT) Pay comes in[cite: 12]. Because extra hours place additional physical and mental demand on a worker, the law mandates a premium rate to ensure fair compensation[cite: 12]. In the Philippines, standard overtime adds a 25% premium, meaning the worker earns 125% (or 1.25 times) their base hourly rate for those extra hours[cite: 12]. Look at our visual breakdown: if an employee earning ₱80/hr works 10 hours, we don't just multiply 10 by ₱80[cite: 12]. We split it! The first 8 hours are regular pay (₱640), and the remaining 2 hours are multiplied by ₱80 and 1.25 (₱200)[cite: 12]. Combining both gives us a total daily wage of ₱840[cite: 12]."

3. Overtime (OT) Pay Principles
  • Definition: Additional compensation for work performed beyond the standard 8-hour workday[cite: 12].
  • The 125% Rule: Regular overtime work is compensated at 1.25 times (125%) the regular hourly rate[cite: 12].
  • Purpose: Fairly compensates workers for physical and mental strain[cite: 12].
$$\text{OT Pay} = \text{Hourly Rate} \times 1.25 \times \text{OT Hours}$$[cite: 12]
10-Hour Workday Breakdown (₱80.00/hr):
  • Regular (8 hrs @ ₱80): $8 \times \text{₱80} = \text{₱640.00}$[cite: 12]
  • OT (2 hrs @ 1.25): $2 \times \text{₱80} \times 1.25 = \text{₱200.00}$[cite: 12]
  • Total Daily Wage: $\mathbf{\text{₱840.00}}$[cite: 12]
4. Understanding Allowances

"In addition to basic wages and overtime, employers often provide allowances—such as rice subsidies, meal stipends, or transportation assistance[cite: 12]. Here is a crucial rule to remember for your financial calculations: allowances are non-wage benefits[cite: 12]. That means you do NOT include allowance amounts when calculating the hourly overtime rate[cite: 12]. You compute regular pay first, add overtime pay second, and then add allowances as a flat addition at the very end[cite: 12]. Keep them separate in your calculation pipeline to avoid errors!"

4. Understanding Allowances
  • Definition: Additional monetary amounts that do not form part of the basic hourly rate[cite: 12].
  • Examples: Meal, transportation, laundry allowances, or rice subsidies[cite: 12].
  • Key Calculation Rule: Allowances are added to the gross pay after regular wages and overtime pay are computed[cite: 12]. They are excluded when computing the 1.25x overtime rate base[cite: 12].
5. Comprehensive Gross Wage Calculation

"Now let's synthesize everything into one master formula[cite: 12]! To calculate an employee's total gross wage, we follow a 3-step sequence: Step 1: Calculate Regular Pay ($40 \text{ hrs} \times \text{Rate}$)[cite: 12]. Step 2: Calculate Overtime Pay ($\text{OT hrs} \times \text{Rate} \times 1.25$)[cite: 12]. Step 3: Add any non-taxable allowances[cite: 12]. Let me walk you through the sample problem: with 40 regular hours at ₱100/hr, we get ₱4,000[cite: 12]. For 4 hours of overtime at ₱100/hr, $4 \times 100 \times 1.25$ equals ₱500[cite: 12]. Adding the ₱200 laundry allowance gives a gross total of ₱4,700[cite: 12]. Always break your solution into these clear steps so you never miss a component."

5. Comprehensive Gross Wage Calculation
$$\text{Gross Wage} = (\text{Reg. Hours} \times \text{Rate}) + (\text{OT Hours} \times \text{Rate} \times 1.25) + \text{Allowances}$$[cite: 12]
Step-by-Step Example:
  • Workweek: 40 Regular Hrs + 4 OT Hrs[cite: 12]
  • Hourly Rate: ₱100.00/hr[cite: 12]
  • Allowance: ₱200.00 laundry allowance[cite: 12]
  1. Regular Pay: $40 \times \text{₱100.00} = \text{₱4,000.00}$[cite: 12]
  2. OT Pay: $4 \times \text{₱100.00} \times 1.25 = \text{₱500.00}$[cite: 12]
  3. Allowance: $\text{₱200.00}$[cite: 12]
  4. Gross Pay: $\text{₱4,000} + \text{₱500} + \text{₱200} = \mathbf{\text{₱4,700.00}}$[cite: 12]
